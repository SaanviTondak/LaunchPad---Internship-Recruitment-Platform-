Executive Summary
The proposed project, a Recruitment Transparency & Feedback Platform, aims to address common hiring challenges faced by students and employers. As competition for jobs grows, candidates (particularly university students and recent graduates) struggle with unclear hiring processes, slow responses from companies, and a lack of actionable feedback. Despite receiving an overwhelming number of applications, employers struggle to attract and secure strong talent.
Our platform is a centralized, crowdsourced hub where users can anonymously share their recruitment experiences. Each company will have quantitative ratings (e.g., fairness, speed, interview difficulty) and qualitative feedback for each stage of the hiring process, per role. Additionally, by aggregating user data, the platform will generate interactive dashboards featuring key recruitment metrics, including Candidate Response Rates, Time-to-Response, Interview-to-Hire Ratios, and emerging skill demands. 
In particular, students are targeted, as they face the greatest hiring uncertainty and rely on internships as key stepping stones for future career success. Using our platform insights, students can optimize their application and interview/assessment strategy. In addition, employers can revise their recruitment process to improve their brand image. Academic institutions and career advisors can further provide more practical guidance and build relevant industry partnerships with fair employers. 
Unlike Telegram groups, Glassdoor, LinkedIn, and Indeed, our solution focuses on the recruitment experience for internships, while streamlining and consolidating information. Through robust user registration, authentication, data collection, and search and filtering functionalities, the system ensures performance, scalability, and usability.
User reported data has the propensity to introduce bias, but a larger dataset is needed for accurate insights, these challenges can be addressed through strategic partnerships and ongoing system improvements. By increasing participation and refining data collection methods, the platform will enhance the reliability of its insights over time.
Our goal is to simplify the hiring process, whether it is by helping students make informed decisions, or empowering employers to improve recruitment practices. By fostering transparency, our team aims to promote a fairer, more efficient, and less hiring environment for all. 


1. Problem Statement
1.1 Increased Job Market Competition 
Breaking into the workforce has never been more challenging. Despite being labeled “entry-level,” many job postings demand prior experience, creating a paradox for new graduates. According to the Wall Street Journal, employers increasingly expect multiple years of experience or advanced degrees for positions once accessible to fresh graduates. LinkedIn reports that 35% of entry-level job postings require at least three years of experience, reinforcing this trend (Ellis 2024). 
As a result, internships have become the primary gateway to employment, yet securing one is highly competitive. In 2023, 8.2 million students sought internships, but 4.6 million (56%) were unsuccessful. Even among those who secured one, only 2.5 million out of 3.6 million internships were considered high-quality, offering structured learning and career development opportunities (BHEF 2024). This intensifies pressure on students to gain multiple internships before graduation, particularly in countries like Singapore, where over half of 2023 graduates completed more than one internship to stay competitive (Channel News Asia 2024).
Beyond fierce competition, students face mass application cycles with minimal transparency. 49% of Gen Z applicants submit more than 50 applications per cycle, while internship postings on Handshake now receive 109 applications on average, up from 43 in 2022 (Forbes 2023, Handshake 2024). This surge has created an oversaturated, high-stakes environment where students struggle to differentiate themselves. A Forbes survey found that 57% of job seekers lack confidence in securing an internship or job that meets their career aspirations, largely due to poor employer communication and unclear hiring expectations (Forbes 2023). 
1.2 Delayed company communication and lack of transparency  
One of the biggest challenges students face is the lack of hiring transparency. Here, transparency refers to the availability of structured, clear, and reliable information about job application processes, selection criteria, and employer expectations. Many candidates struggle to grasp which qualifications matter most, how applications are evaluated, and why they are rejected. Furthermore, hiring timelines are often unclear, leaving candidates uncertain about when to expect a response or whether they have been rejected altogether. 
This lack of transparency is further compounded by delayed communication from employers. According to the 2023 Global Candidate Experience (CandE 2023) Benchmark Research Report:
36% of candidates wait over a month to hear back from employers.
Only 5% of applicants are explicitly informed when rejected.
Only 16% are given a clear hiring timeline. 
Applicants often continue waiting indefinitely or prematurely assume rejection, leading to missed opportunities on both sides. Talented candidates may withdraw their applications or accept competing offers before receiving a decision, making it harder for employers to secure top secret. Alarmingly, one in three students choose not to apply for internships because they perceive the process as overly complex and uncertain (BHEF 2024).
Beyond delayed responses, candidates also face a lack of meaningful feedback. While only 22% received any assessment-related insights, 77.3% of recipients found it vague and unhelpful (Anders 2021). 
Without structured feedback, students struggle to identify areas for improvement, leading to repeated mistakes and a prolonged job search. This growing frustration is reflected in the rising Candidate Resentment Rate, which tracks dissatisfaction due to hiring experiences (Figure 1.2.1). Since 2020, resentment among job seekers has increased, signaling a broader shift in how applicants perceive the hiring process. The Candidate Experience Benchmark Research & Awards Program cites delayed responses, unclear hiring timelines, and lack of feedback as key drivers of this shift.

1.3 Increased Demand for Hiring Insights 
Students struggle to find reliable, structured information to guide their applications. Many are forced to navigate Telegram groups, Glassdoor reviews, LinkedIn posts, and word-of-mouth to understand what employers expect. However, insights are often outdated, fragmented, or anecdotal, making it difficult to extract actionable strategies.
Without structured information, students face the following challenges:
Uncertainty in application strategy: Many students apply to internships and jobs without fully understanding employers expectations leading to misaligned applications and missed opportunities. A lack of transparency in assessment formats, response timelines, and qualification criteria leaves students second-guessing their approach.
Inability to gauge competition: Unlike in academic settings where students have a clear benchmark for performance, the job market is opaque, students have no way of knowing what makes their competitors stand out. Many students keep their experiences and successes private, leaving others completely in the dark about what a “strong” candidate looks like for a particular role. This lack of visibility makes it difficult for students to benchmark their own skills and credentials effectively.
Reliance on guesswork: The absence of clear hiring standards forces students to learn through trial and error, often wasting months applying to roles they are under- or overqualified for. Without employer feedback, students are prone to repeat mistakes. 
Hiring inefficiencies for both candidates and employers: Without a proper understanding of the hiring process, students may apply blindly, leading to an influx of ill-suited applications for companies. This not only increases rejection rates but also wastes resources on both sides. 
The lack of structured hiring insights contributes to frustration, inefficiencies, and wasted effort for students.  
1.4 Our Solution: A Crowdsourced Feedback Platform
We propose a crowdsourced feedback platform that provides real-time, structured, and community-driven hiring insights. The platform aims to: 
Empower job seekers by reducing frustrations related to hiring certainty. 
Encourage skill development by enabling candidates to learn from shared experiences and improve future applications. 
Our platform will serve as a centralized hub where candidates can share and access firsthand hiring experiences, addressing communication gaps and lack of hiring transparency through three key features: 
Feature 1: Anonymous Experience Sharing on Recruitment Fairness and Process Speed
1a) Quantitative Company Ratings   
Students can rate companies quantitatively based on the different aspects of the recruitment process such as fairness, speed, difficulty of interview, time punctuality etc. For instance, they can rate how long was the recruitment process (eg.1-2 stages, 3-4 stages)  and how many days (eg. a few days, within 1 month) a company takes to respond from the options. Our team found this function crucial for applicants because in addition to the statistics already mentioned, there are also numerous anecdotal reports from colleagues that further highlight the issues. For example, some students have shared experiences where companies have not shown up for scheduled interviews, and some companies have simply gone silent even after the interview process. This kind of malpractice deepens the overall sense of uncertainty and mistrust toward the hiring process. Our platform’s feature of Company Ratings on Recruitment process and Process Speed addresses the issues. 
By allowing users to share and rate their experiences, we provide a transparent benchmark for candidate experience. This not only helps future applicants to make more informed decisions but also pressures companies to adhere to more professional and timely hiring practices.
1b) Qualitative Feedback
Users can explain the reason behind each rating as well. They can submit detailed interview reviews anonymously, covering: interview difficulty, commonly asked questions, assessment formats, and employer responsiveness and professionalism.  

This feature ensures that all applicants– whether successful, rejected, or left without a response– contribute to a collective knowledge base. 

Successful candidates can provide insights on what helped them stand out, as well as a thorough insight on the start to end of the application process. 
Rejected candidates can further contribute feedback on their experiences, to help future applicants prepare strategically. 
Candidates with no responses can log their experience, helping others gauge realistic response times and assess company communication practices. 

Applicant anonymity will be maintained to ensure user protection and encourage active participation for honest, constructive feedback. 
Feature 2: Data-Driven Hiring Pattern Insights
Our platform collects and aggregates data from thousands of users to discover hidden trends and patterns in the hiring landscape. Upon creating an account, users will have to provide key details such as their university, major, and field of study. They will also have the option to share additional information such as their GPA, skills, previous work experience, and certifications. This anonymized data, combined with company insights gathered through student-submitted reviews, will enable us to generate a comprehensive and actionable dataset that benefits both job seekers and employers.
By leveraging this data, we will create interactive dashboards that provide a view of the recruitment process, and some of this data will also be easily viewed in the reviews section. Users will be able to analyze various recruitment metrics, such as application success rates, employer response times, interview-to-hire ratios, and most demanded skills across industries. These insights will help applicants make more informed and strategic career decisions, allowing them to better understand which qualifications, skills, and educational backgrounds are most valued for specific roles. Users will also be able to filter the data by various criteria, such as industry, job function, and recruitment stage, to gain a more personalized understanding of where they stand in the hiring process.

Our platform is designed to cater the news of several groups, who stand to gain substantial benefits from increased transparency and data-driven insights.
1.5.1 Students Seeking Internships and Entry-level job opportunities 
Students looking for information on potential employers to make informed decisions about applications and prepare effectively for interviews and other assessments. 
Enhanced interview preparation: 
Access to detailed, anonymised interview experiences enables applicants to tailor their preparation. By understanding common questions and assessment tools used by companies, candidates can approach interviews with greater confidence and improved performance. 
Access to Transparent and Reliable information: 
Rather than digging through messy, fragmented online forums or unreliable chat groups, users can gain a centralised source of verified information. This reduces the uncertainty and stress associated with the job search process, which negatively impacts their mental health.
Informed Application Decisions:
Applicants can compare which academic backgrounds, previous work and volunteer/extra-curricular experiences are most aligned with specific job roles. This can enable applicants to strategically apply to positions they are most suitable for, increasing hiring efficiency. 
Data on hiring patterns and company preferences empowers students to target employers whose recruitment culture and practices align with their values and career aspirations after reading the reviews. 
1.5.2 Potential Employers
A well-structured hiring process is essential for attracting top talent. Yet, only 40% of HR professionals actively measure candidate satisfaction, leaving many organizations unaware of the inefficiencies in their recruitment strategies. 

Data-driven recruitment optimization: 

By leveraging our platform’s comprehensive employer ratings and hiring insights, companies can identify process bottlenecks, analyze candidate sentiment, and benchmark hiring practices against industry standards. With these insights, organizations can refine their recruitment workflows, optimize hiring timelines, and enhance applicant engagement strategies, ultimately fostering a more efficient, structured, and equitable hiring process.

Enhanced employer branding: 

Candidates are significantly more likely to apply for and accept offers from organizations that demonstrate structured, transparent, and fair hiring practices. Our platform provides data-driven insights into candidate experience trends, enabling companies to proactively enhance transparency, streamline hiring interactions, and build trust with job seekers. By cultivating a positive and candidate-centric hiring environment, organizations can differentiate themselves in a competitive job market, attract high-caliber applicants, and increase offer acceptance rates.

Reduced financial loss:

Investing in hiring transparency and candidate experience is not just about better recruitment. it’s a business imperative that directly impacts organization revenue. According to Deloitte, a media company lost $5.4 million in revenue after rejected candidates disengaged from its services due to a poor hiring experience (Deloitte 2017). Negative word-of-mouth further amplifies these losses, discouraging future applicants and reducing customer loyalty. By utilizing our platform’s recruitment analytics, companies can minimize such financial risks.

1.5.3 Educational Staff and Career Advisors
Universities and career advisors can utilize the insights on company hiring practices to guide students more efficiently. 
Data-driven career guidance: 
With access to current trends and detailed feedback on hiring practices, advisors can offer more tailored recommendations to students. 
Improved employer partnerships: 
By understanding which companies maintain high standards in candidate engagement, educational institutions can forge strategic partnerships with top employers. This enhances student placement opportunities and fosters industry-academic collaboration.
1.6 Differentiating from the Competition 
1.6.1 Telegram Chat Groups 
Telegram chat groups are informal discussion forums, where job seekers share hiring timelines, assessment formats, and general job search feedback. Although these groups provide a community-driven platform, they are unmoderated. Conversations are scattered, unstructured, and easily lost over time as can be seen in Appendix 1, making it difficult for job seekers to extract actionable and reliable insights. However, our platform focuses on centralizing hiring insights and offering structured reviews for different application processes. Therefore, applicants can gain comprehensive and data-driven insights immediately. 
1.6.2 Glassdoor
Glassdoor focuses on extensive job listings and detailed reviews of company work life including employee benefits, culture and overall job satisfaction as can be seen in Appendix 2. However, our platform is focused on the hiring process itself. Our platform is dedicated exclusively to reviewing the recruitment experience. Users share insights about interview formats, coding assessments, personality tests, interview questions, scheduling punctuality, and overall fairness in the recruitment process, which is not the primary focus on Glassdoor. By centering on the recruitment experience, applicants can learn from others and gain actionable insights. 
1.6.3 LinkedIn
LinkedIn is primarily a professional networking site with a secondary emphasis on job listings and career development content. It allows users to connect with colleagues, classmates, industry experts and recruiters. Thus, as seen in Appendix 3, LinkedIn lists jobs and offers an easy apply feature, allowing candidates to apply quickly using their LinkedIn profiles. In contrast, our platform is dedicated to sharing verified recruitment experiences and fairness ratings. Rather than focusing on broad networking or job discovery, we highlight how companies conduct interviews and assessments and how they communicate with candidates. Although you can find some interview-related posts and employer updates, there is no dedicated feature for in depth, crowdsourced interview reviews. Thus, anonymity does not exist in LinkedIn. This contrasts with our platform’s approach, which allows users to share interview feedback anonymously, ensuring candid and unbiased insights into the hiring process without risking personal or professional repercussions. 
1.6.4 Indeed
Indeed is a job aggregator, pulling listings from various sources, allowing users to post resumes , search for positions and read basic company reviews (Appendix 4). Again, Indeed’s user feedback often focuses on general workplace impressions such as company culture or pay, work life balances etc rather than on the specific stages of the hiring process. As a result, candidates seeking detailed interview questions or processes usually won’t find the same level of depth that an interview-focused platform can provide.
In conclusion, while existing platforms like Glassdoor, LinkedIn and Indeed focus on company ratings covering work life balance, salary, culture, job security and on aggregating job listings, our platform sets itself apart by focusing on the interview and recruitment process.

3. Data-Driven Hiring Pattern Insights 
 
3.1 Introduction

In today’s competitive talent market, it is of utmost importance for students to understand subtle hiring trends. Often, the requirements that companies are looking for and the invisible ‘glass ceilings’ that preclude students from certain roles are not clear to students. This is a big source of consternation for students, who are left scratching their heads as to why they were rejected from a particular role. Our platform aims to aggregate vast amounts of anonymized recruitment data to provide insights that are actionable. These insights can empower job candidates and employers to make data-driven decisions regarding their applications and reduce the confusion around the hiring process.
 
We will collect anonymized data from thousands of applications, through self-reporting from companies and applicants, capturing several key metrics to create a comprehensive dashboard that can reflect several key analytical metrics. This dashboard can be customized and filtered to reflect different segments of the job market.
 
3.2 Collected Data
 
We will be collecting several data points from the hiring process by parsing applicant data and hires made by the company. This can be further segmented into specific sections.

Student Data 
Educational Background: Includes university, major(s), year of study, and GPA. This will be collected from the applicants when they register for an account.
Application Stage Tracking: Record whether applicants were shortlisted, placed on hold, advanced to interviews, received an offer, or were rejected. This information will be collected from applicants self-reporting.
Employment History & Experience: Previous internships/work experience, and certifications held. 
Skills & Qualifications: Technical and soft skills, industry-relevant certifications. 
Self-Reported Candidate Feedback: Experience with specific hiring processes, interview difficulty ratings, and employer communication. 
 
Company Data 
Job Postings & Open Positions: Number of available roles, job descriptions, and skill requirements.
Application Volume Per Role: Expected/Realized number of applicants per open position. 

Company data will be collected from the students, self-reporting of their experience with the application process. We will group their responses together by company and further by role to determine the company data that we have outlined.
 
3.3 Data Aggregation
Through our comprehensive data collection, we will create a data pool that we can use to create aggregate data and advanced analytics to further improve insights into the hiring process. These aggregated statistics will be displayed in both the dashboard and in the review section. 

Key Aggregate Insights: 

Recruitment process
Candidate Response Rate (CRR): Percentage of applicants receiving a response (acceptance/rejection) within a standard timeframe.
Time-to-Response (TTR): Average time taken for an employer to acknowledge an application (i.e. rejection email). 
Offer Turnaround Time (OTT): Number of days between a candidate’s final interview and job offer issuance. 
Interview-to-Hire Ratio (IHR): Number of interviews conducted per successful hire.

Candidate Selection & Hiring Patterns 
Top Applicant Demographics: Breakdown of educational backgrounds, experience levels, and skill sets of successful hires.
Employer Preference Index (EPI): Weighted ranking of universities, certifications, and skills that appear most frequently in successful candidate profiles.

Candidate Experience & Employer Brand Perception 
Recruitment Experience Score (RES): Aggregated candidate rating of hiring fairness, professionalism, and clarity of the process. 
Employer Candidate Sentiment Index (ECSI): Measures the employer’s reputation based on candidate reviews and hiring feedback. 
Candidate Net Promoter Score (cNPS): Percentage of candidates willing to recommend a company’s hiring process to peers.

Job Market Competitiveness Metrics 
Job Function Competitiveness Index (JFCI): Ranking of roles based on application volume-to-hire ratio (e.g. data science vs. marketing vs. consulting).
Emerging Skills Demand (ESD): Identifies in-demand technical and soft skills based on job descriptions and hiring trends.
Internship-to-Full-Time Conversion Rate (IFTCR): Ranking of companies, based on the percentage of interns receiving return offers. 

3.4 Data Segmentation
 
Once we have aggregated the data, we can now segment it into specific categories to create better filters to improve the user experience. Users can filter their dashboard to better understand the hiring landscape. Users can segment their dashboard by two broad categories, user type and recruitment stage.

3.4.1 Segmentation by User Type 

Educational Background: Filters by major, university, and GPA range to determine which roles align best with specific academic backgrounds. 
Hiring competitiveness: Uses Candidate Response Rate (CRR) and Interview-to-hire ratio (IHR) to compare hiring likelihoods across industries and companies. 
Top Skills & Experience: Uses Emerging Skills Demand (ESD) index to identify in-demand skills. 
 
Users can segment the data for specific jobs or roles by applicants’ educational background and can filter based on the metrics that we collect and aggregate from the users. This can allow the user to analyze trends like which majors are most suited for specific roles, thereby ensuring that they can better understand which roles are suited for their skillset.

3.4.2 Segmentation by Recruitment Stage 
 
The data has also been categorized by where candidates are in the recruitment stage. This segmentation can help to elucidate the hiring process for applicants, where they are often unaware of how many people companies typically shortlist and are left in the dark as to why they may have been rejected or why they have not received a response yet.
 
3.5 Future Direction and Continuous Improvement
 
In the future, we look to continually improve upon our processes of data collection as well as the quality of aggregated data that we can derive insights from. As the system gains traction and we are able to collect data over a longer period, we can enhance the granularity of our insights. In future iterations of our system, we will explore advanced techniques to analyze unstructured data, such as candidate profiles and interviewer feedback, allowing us to create more insightful recommendations for our users. 
4. User Requirements Analysis
4.1 Functional Requirements

F-REQ-001 – User Registration, Profile Setup & Authentication
Requirement Description:
Registration & Verification:
Users register with a username, valid school email (e.g., @nus.edu.sg, @ntu.edu.sg), and password.
A verification email is sent immediately, and the account is activated only after the user clicks the verification link.
Profile Setup:
During registration, users are prompted to optionally provide additional profile details (university, major, year of study, GPA, work/internship experience, skills, certifications) to enable personalized insights and enrich aggregated data.
Authentication:
The system supports secure login, password reset (via email), and logout across devices.
Acceptance Criteria:
Only recognized school emails are accepted, and duplicate registrations are prevented.
A verification email is sent and must be used to activate the account.
Users can optionally complete their profile with additional academic/professional details.
Verified users can log in securely; incorrect credentials trigger clear error messages.
Priority: High

F-REQ-002 – Quantitative Feedback and Ratings
Requirement Description:
Submission Interface:
Users rate the Overall Candidate Experience of the recruitment process using a predefined 1–5 scale based on these specific criteria:
Fairness of the Hiring Process
Speed of Employer Response
Interview Difficulty/Punctuality
Quantitative Options:
Predefined options are provided for each criterion to ensure consistency across reviews.
The system collates these ratings to compute average scores for each criterion, as well as an overall rating per company.
Integration and Display:
Quantitative scores are stored and displayed alongside the associated qualitative feedback.
Company profiles show aggregated scores and sample user comments to provide a full picture of the recruitment process.
Acceptance Criteria:
Users can submit numeric ratings for each of the four defined criteria.
The system calculates and displays average scores for each company.
Both the numeric ratings and linked qualitative comments are searchable and filterable.
Priority: High 

F-REQ-003 – Qualitative Feedback for Recruitment Process Reviews
Requirement Description:
Submission Interface:
Users provide detailed, text-based feedback explaining why they gave specific quantitative ratings.
This qualitative feedback covers insights such as personal experiences during the interview, examples of employer responsiveness or delays, and any context that influenced their view of the recruitment process.
Content Details:
The text feedback should be tied directly to the quantitative ratings submitted.
It may include narratives covering interview experiences, perceived fairness, communication effectiveness, and overall impressions.
Content Management:
Users can edit or delete their qualitative feedback after submission.
Integration:
This qualitative data is stored alongside the corresponding quantitative ratings for a comprehensive review display on company profiles.
Acceptance Criteria:
Users can submit a free-text explanation linked to their quantitative scores.
Submitted qualitative feedback is searchable and can be edited or removed by the original author.
The qualitative comments are displayed with the quantitative ratings to provide context.
Priority: High

F-REQ-004 – Data-Driven Hiring Insights Dashboard
Requirement Description:
Data Aggregation & Visualization:
The system aggregates anonymized data from user submissions (reviews, ratings, and profile details) to compute key recruitment metrics, including:
Recruitment Process Metrics:
Candidate Response Rate (CRR), Time-to-Response (TTR), Offer Turnaround Time (OTT), Interview-to-Hire Ratio (IHR)
Candidate Experience Metrics:
Recruitment Experience Score (RES), Employer Candidate Sentiment Index (ECSI), Candidate Net Promoter Score (cNPS)
Job Market Competitiveness Metrics:
Job Function Competitiveness Index (JFCI), Emerging Skills Demand (ESD)
Candidate Selection Patterns:
Top Applicant Demographics and Employer Preference Index (EPI)
Interactive Dashboard:
The platform provides an interactive dashboard where these metrics are presented via charts, graphs, and tables.
Users can customize and filter the dashboard by industry, job role, recruitment stage, and applicant profile (e.g., educational background).
Acceptance Criteria:
Aggregated data is updated regularly and presented in visual formats.
Users can apply multiple filters to segment the insights.
Key metrics and trends are clearly displayed for actionable insights.
Priority:
Low for the initial Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
Medium as the platform scales

F-REQ-005 – Comprehensive Data Collection & Profile Data Analytics
Requirement Description:
Student Data Collection:
Capture applicant details such as educational background (university, major, GPA), application stage (shortlisted, interviewed, offered, rejected), work experience, skills, and self-reported hiring process feedback.
Company Data Collection:
Gather self-reported company information regarding job postings, available roles, and application volumes (via candidate submissions).
Data Segmentation:
Segment collected data by user type (e.g., educational background) and recruitment stage to enable detailed filtering and analysis.
Acceptance Criteria:
All relevant data fields are captured during registration and feedback submission.
Data is anonymized and aggregated to produce reliable hiring metrics.
Users can view segmented data in the analytics dashboard.
Priority: Medium

F-REQ-006 – Search & Filter Functionality
Requirement Description:
Search Interface:
Implement robust search and filtering across all sections of the platform, including interview experiences, work/internship reviews, company ratings, and dashboard analytics.
Filtering Criteria:
Allow filtering by company, job role, industry, recruitment stage, educational background, and other relevant dimensions.
Acceptance Criteria:
Users can quickly retrieve relevant content based on multi-criteria searches.
The search results update dynamically as filters are applied.
Priority: Medium

F-REQ-007 – Content Moderation & Reporting
Requirement Description:
Reporting Mechanism:
Provide users with the ability to report inappropriate, misleading, or offensive content.
Moderation Panel:
Administrators have a dedicated interface to review reported content, flag issues, and remove problematic submissions if necessary.
User Notifications:
Users receive notifications if their submissions are flagged or removed.
Acceptance Criteria:
A reporting feature is available on all user-generated content.
The moderation panel supports efficient review and action on flagged content.
Notifications are sent to users regarding the status of their submissions.
Priority: Medium

4.2 Non-Functional Requirements

NFR-REQ-001 – Performance
Requirement Description:
The website needs to deliver rapid response times to make sure that our different target users have overall smooth experience.
Typical user interactions include having to search for company reviews, submitting feedback forms on the hiring process, and loading the data analytics dashboard.
Acceptance Criteria:
Search & Retrieval: 90% of all search requests and page loads must complete within 2 seconds under normal load (up to 100 concurrent users).
Data Analytics Dashboard: Visualizations (charts and statistics) should render within 5 seconds under normal load.
Peak Load Performance: Under peak load (e.g., up to 500 concurrent users), at least 80% of requests must complete within 3 seconds.
Priority:
High – A slow or unresponsive system undermines the entire platform’s utility.

NFR-REQ-002 – Scalability
Requirement Description:
The system must gracefully handle growing amounts of user data and increased concurrent user sessions without significant degradation in performance.
This applies to both user-generated content (e.g., reviews, ratings) and data-analytics computations.
Acceptance Criteria:
The architecture should support horizontal scaling (e.g., adding more servers or containers) to accommodate at least 10x the initial user base with minimal refactoring.
Performance metrics (e.g., average response time, throughput) should not degrade by more than 20% when the user base doubles.
Database or storage services must be able to handle an exponentially increasing volume of reviews (from thousands to millions) without impacting core read/write operations.
Priority:
Medium – The platform must ensure smooth growth but can optimize incrementally.

NFR-REQ-003 – Portability
Requirement Description:
The system should run on a range of hardware, operating systems, and browsers to maximize accessibility for all intended users.
The platform should remain consistent across desktop, laptop, tablet, and mobile devices so that users can use it on their different devices according to their convenience. 
Acceptance Criteria:
Supported OS: Latest versions of Windows, macOS, and popular Linux distributions.
Supported Browsers: Latest two major releases of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Microsoft Edge.
Device Responsiveness: The user interface (UI) maintains core functionality and readability across desktop and mobile form factors.
Priority:
Low 

NFR-REQ-004 – Reliability
Requirement Description:
The system must remain stable and handle critical failures gracefully.
Critical failures are those that cause data corruption, complete service outages, or prevent essential functionalities (e.g., user login, review submission).
Acceptance Criteria:
MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures): There should at least be 30 days in between critical failures in production. 
Error Handling: There are fallback messages or clear messages of indication  (e.g., “Please retry later” or “Service temporarily unavailable”) to the user in the event of partial failures. This will ensure that users do not get confused in case of system failure. 
Critical Data Integrity: No user data (such as reviews, ratings, or personal profiles) is lost or corrupted during minor service disruptions. 
Priority:
High – Consistent reliability is crucial for user trust and adoption.

NFR-REQ-005 – Maintainability
Requirement Description:
To facilitate quick troubleshooting and updates the system should be developed in a modular, well-documented way.  
Developers should be able to isolate and fix bugs without impacting the entire platform.
Acceptance Criteria:
MTTR (Mean Time to Repair): High-priority issues or critical bugs can be diagnosed and resolved (or hotfixed) within 4 hours on average.
Code Documentation: Core components (e.g., review submission, analytics engine) must include inline comments and API documentation to streamline the onboarding of new developers.
Configuration Management: A version-controlled configuration (e.g., Infrastructure-as-Code) must exist so that changes to environments (staging/production) can be rolled back easily.
Priority:
Medium – Efficient maintenance ensures long-term sustainability and reduces operational costs.

NFR-REQ-006 – Security
Requirement Description:
The system must protect sensitive data (e.g., user emails, password hashes, aggregated hiring data) and guard against unauthorized access or malicious activities 
User anonymity must be guaranteed when sharing experiences or ratings.
Acceptance Criteria:
Data Encryption: All user data in transit and at rest must use industry-standard algorithms.

5. Limitations
While our platform provides valuable hiring insights, the following limitations influence the accuracy and scope of the data: 
5.1 Self-Reported Data Bias: 
The majority of our data is crowdsourced, and applicant-reported data may be subjective and incomplete. This could create potential bias in our hiring trend analysis. Rejected or unresponsive candidates may provide lower ratings or more negative feedback, while successful applicants might emphasize positive aspects. Although our platform collects multiple data points to mitigate individual biases, some responses may still reflect personal sentiment. 
Furthermore, we cannot independently verify whether users have actually gone through a company’s hiring process. Like other review-based platforms, our system relies on trust-based submissions. To address the concerns, our platform encourages detailed, constructive feedback and implements the content moderation and reporting mechanism to maintain its review quality. 

5.1.2 Dependence on data volume for accuracy: 
The reliability and usefulness of our insights heavily depends on a large volume of data from both applicants and employers. A higher number of submissions ensures more accurate hiring trends, whereas a limited dataset may create skewed results. Insufficient data for certain companies or industries may result in hiring trends that are not fully reflective of the broader job market. Thus, the inconsistencies in user submissions such as missing details or differing interpretations of hiring experiences could also impact the clarity of aggregated insights. To improve accuracy, we aim to encourage active participation by forming strategic partnerships with universities and career centers, ensuring an influx of verified user contributions. By continuously refining our data collection approach, we strive to provide meaningful insights for job seekers.
